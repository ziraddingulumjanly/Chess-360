Documentation

Controls, board annotations and the technical details behind Chess 360.

Play against Stockfish

Select Easy, Medium, Hard, or Expert, choose your color under Side & time control, and press New game. Click a piece and then a highlighted destination, or drag it. Stockfish calculates the opponent's move locally in your browser.

There is no online matchmaking or account system. The computer opponent is the full Stockfish 19 WebAssembly engine.

Board and controls

  • Undo: take back the last move, including the computer's reply when appropriate.
  • Flip: rotate the board.
  • Resign: end the active game with a loss.
  • Copy PGN: copy a text record of played moves.
  • Move history: use the arrow buttons to review earlier positions.
  • Arrows: right-drag from one square to another to annotate a line. On touch devices, enable Arrows mode and tap both squares. Use × to clear annotations.
  • Settings: change board colors, move markers, coordinates and sounds.

Clocks and board sizing

Untimed games hide the clock display. Selecting Blitz or Rapid displays both countdown clocks beneath the board. The board size is bounded by the visible screen height to keep all 64 squares visible without needing to scroll down.

Local data and privacy

Game history, statistics, and settings are kept in your browser's localStorage. The application does not create accounts, upload game history, or set tracking cookies. Clearing browser site data will erase local records. See the Privacy Policy and Cookie Policy for details.

How it works

chess.js validates the rules, including castling, promotion, en passant and draw conditions. Stockfish 19 computes computer moves in a Web Worker using WebAssembly and NNUE evaluation. Lucide supplies UI icons. This version runs without application dependencies or cloud services.

The full engine requires an HTTPS or localhost origin and cross-origin isolation security headers; its first-time download is approximately 99 MB. See Licenses & sources for attribution.
